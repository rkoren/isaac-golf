# CLAUDE.md — isaac-golf

Side project: teach a stick-figure agent to swing golf clubs and play a designed course, using RL in NVIDIA Isaac Lab. Primary goal is **learning RL in simulated spaces** — a working, ugly swing beats a pretty stalled project.

# Leave the README plain

## Architecture (three phases — do not blend them)

**Phase 1 — Learn the swing (Isaac Lab, massively parallel RL)**
Stick figure + club welded to hands + ball on tee, flat ground. Goal: hit far and straight.

**Phase 2 — Analytic ball flight**
Do NOT simulate ball flight in PhysX (no Magnus effect; sub-ms club impact is numerically unreliable). At impact, read clubhead state → feed an analytic flight model (launch speed/angle, spin, drag + Magnus lift — standard golf-physics equations) → landing position.
The **launch itself is already analytic** (Phase 1 env): ball speed = **smash factor** × clubhead speed into the face, the way launch monitors and sims report it. Smash is 1.47 for a centred driver strike and falls off with distance from the sweet spot (~1.2 for a 2 cm toe miss, ~1.0 topped or skulled); start direction is 85% face normal, 15% club path. The sim ball itself still flies straight with the collider's flat face (it feeds the observations, so it stays as trained); the env logs face and path at impact (`Metrics/face_deg`, `path_deg`, `face_to_path_deg`, + = right) and `record_swings.py` labels each shot from face-to-path (approximate bands: ±2° straight, 2–6° fade/draw, beyond that slice/hook).
**The flight model (`flight/`, 2026-11-18)** turns each strike into a real ball flight, metrics only (no reward uses it yet; `env.flight`, on by default, ~6% training time):
- *Launch angle:* the collider face has no loft, so each club's `loft_deg` (`clubs.py`) tilts the face normal up about the toe-heel line that is horizontal at address; dynamic loft, attack angle and spin loft then come from the sim's head pose and velocity at impact. The start line is the env's `offline`.
- *Spin:* the axis is perpendicular to the plane of club path and face normal (the D-plane), so a face open to the path tilts it right and the ball curves right; the rate is k × club speed × sin(3D spin loft), the face sliding across the ball, with k per club (`trackman` row in `clubs.py`: driver 112, 7-iron 199, PW 208 rpm per mph) from TrackMan's 2023 PGA and LPGA averages (`flight/data.py`); within 20% of every club on both tours (driver ±15%).
- *Flight:* gravity, drag CD0 + CD1·S and Magnus lift CL_MAX(1 − e^(−S/CL_S)) on spin ratio S = rω/v, spin decaying with τ 25 s, RK4, from the ball's tee height to the ground; no bounce or roll. The four coefficients are fitted to the PGA table (`scripts/fit_flight.py`); the LPGA table, held out, comes in within 5% on carry, 8 yd on apex, 4° on landing angle (PGA: within 11% carry, the PW and driver short). A Reynolds-number drag term fit PGA better and LPGA worse: overfit, dropped. Our golfer's ~85 mph ball is below every table row (lowest: LPGA PW, 88 mph, within 4%), so its carries are extrapolated.
- Logs `Flight/carry_yd`, `side_yd` (where it lands, + right), `curve_yd` (off the start line), `apex_yd`, `spin_rpm`, `axis_deg` (+ curves right), `launch_deg`, `attack_deg`, `dynamic_loft_deg`, `spin_loft_deg`; `record_swings.py` prints them and the viewer shows them per swing. Tests: `uv run python -m unittest flight.test_flight -v`.
`balfix3` model_2195, measured at impact: attack −9.4°, dynamic loft −5.9° (the face points down even with the driver's 10.5°), launch −6.4°: every drive goes into the ground (carry 1 yd), so its slice (face-to-path +6.7°, 70% slices, 30% fades) never shows. With a level attack and 13° of loft, the same face and path would carry ~80 yd and slice ~6 yd. Clubs = different welded geometries + loft angles; policy conditions on club ID.
**Clubs** live in `envs/golf/clubs.py` (driver, 7iron, pw: length, shaft angle, head height, stance, smash ceiling, look). Each has its own asset (`assets/golfer_<club>.usda`, built by `make_golfer_asset.py`) and solved address (`envs/golf/address/<club>.json`, written by `solve_address.py --club`). Pick one per run with `env.club=7iron`; one policy per club for now, club-ID conditioning later. Head looks are visual meshes; the strike uses an invisible box collider.

**Phase 3 — Play a course (hierarchical)**
Designed course as USD terrain/heightmap. High-level layer (heuristic or simple policy) picks club + aim + power target per shot; low-level swing policy executes. Never attempt tee-to-hole end-to-end with one policy.

## Phase 1 environment spec

**Starting skeleton:** fork an existing Isaac Lab humanoid task (the Humanoid locomotion env, or the AMP humanoid example if going the motion-imitation route later). Strip to our needs rather than building from zero.

**Body, as measured from the USD (not assumed):** 16 rigid bodies, 15 joints, **21 actuated DoF**, root is a free-floating `torso`. Arms attach to `torso`, so torso joints never reposition the hands — spine tilt at address comes from root orientation set at reset. Coil budget is small: waist twist ±45°, bend −75°/+30°, pelvis side-bend ±35°, shoulders 2 DoF each, elbows 1 DoF, **no wrists** (an optional lead wrist hinge exists, see below). Expect an arm-dominant half-coil swing. Golfer stands ~1.6 m, so scale the club (~0.9 m, not a real 1.14 m driver). Both hands *can* reach one grip point (31% of the lead hand's 0.52 m³ workspace, 1.21 m arc) — reachability was never the issue, the loop was.

**Agent body:** simplified humanoid articulation (USD or MJCF/URDF). Start with the stock Isaac Lab humanoid (~21–28 DoF) even though it's more than a "stick figure" — a working asset beats a custom one. Reduce DoF later only if training struggles. Club: rigid capsule/box welded to both hands as a fixed joint. **Grasping is out of scope — never make the agent learn grip.**

**Observations (per env):**
- Joint positions + velocities (normalized)
- Torso/pelvis orientation (quat or 6D) + linear/angular velocity
- Clubhead position + velocity relative to ball
- Ball position (and velocity once struck, Phase 1 only)
- Target direction vector + commanded power (for conditioning, Phase 2+)
- Phase/time-in-episode scalar

**Actions:** joint torques or position targets (Isaac Lab humanoid examples use torque control — keep their choice); action dim = DoF of the articulation.
Measured 2026-10-30: the implicit actuators also carry PD stiffness (10–20 N·m/rad on arms and waist) toward position targets of **joint zero** — the stock standing pose, not the address. With zero action the golfer springs out of address on the first step (clubhead 4–16 mph, gravity off). Every torque the policy outputs fights that spring. Open question, not yet changed.
**Faster than a human (measured 2026-10-30):** the 7-iron hits 91 mph at impact with the lead hand at 35 mph (real golfers ~20) only 0.27 s after address (real swings ~1 s). With no wrists, head speed has to come from hands moving faster than a person can, and the actuators give full torque at any joint speed. Watch `Metrics/impact_hand_speed_mph` and `Metrics/impact_time` next to clubhead speed; a speed gain with hands far above ~20 mph is the actuators, not technique. Candidate fix: a torque–speed curve, now available as the muscle model below.
**Muscle model (`env.muscle=true`, off by default):** Hill's force–velocity curve on every joint, so torque falls as a joint speeds up: × (1 − x)/(1 + x/0.25), x = shortening speed / vmax, vmax 20–30 rad/s per joint (`MUSCLE_VMAX` in `golf_env_cfg.py`). Applied every physics substep in `_apply_action`. Not `DCMotorCfg`: past its no-load speed a DC motor brakes, which would punish an outer joint being whipped faster by the body behind it (the kinetic chain); muscle just goes slack. Measured 2026-10-30: hands drop to human speed or below (9–16 mph), strikes get cleaner (smash 1.28–1.31), clubhead falls to 40–49 mph, and the swing stays short (impact ~0.3 s). It still kneels at the finish (torso 0.70–0.84 m). Another 300 iterations (2026-10-31) raised the finish to ~1.0 m, but as a wide lunge with the trail foot slid back, not a golf finish.
**Joint damping is a hidden speed cap (measured 2026-10-31):** the stock actuators carry 5 N·m·s/rad on waist, shoulders, pelvis and hips (~10–50× human). In the downswing it burns 74% of the muscle work, and at impact it out-pulls the muscles, so a longer backswing only feeds the damper; that, not the reward, is why every swing stopped at the 1.2 m `backswing_cap`. `env.damping_scale=0.1` (`DAMPING` in `golfer.py`, default 1.0): clubhead 40 → 68 mph with hands at a human 22 mph, the hip and arm springs start storing and returning energy, but the backswing only grows to 1.28 m (the arms now hit their muscle vmax) and it falls 98% of the time, since the leg dampers had been balancing it for free. Discount γ 0.995 changed nothing about timing.
**Balance at low damping (2026-10-31):** resuming that run with `env.stand_weight=10 env.feet_slip_weight=10` (plus `aim_sigma=15`, `finish_low=0.8`) brings falls from 98% to 19% at 71 mph (ball 89 mph, ~1° offline), still improving. Bracing the legs instead (`env.leg_damping_scale=1.0`: hips, knees, ankles at stock damping, no balance rewards) stands as often (16%) but costs ~11 mph. Best driver recipe so far: muscles, `damping_scale=0.1`, balance rewards.
**Lead wrist hinge (`env.wrist=true`, 2026-10-31):** the weld becomes a revolute joint `lead_wrist` about the club's toe-heel axis (the swing plane at address), −25°/+70° from the address angle, weak on purpose (10 N·m vs 67.5 for the arms). Asset `golfer_<club>_wrist.usda` from `make_golfer_asset.py --club <club> --wrist` (driver only so far); 22 actions, 84 observations; address unchanged. It is what makes a long backswing pay: from scratch at low damping the backswing grows to 1.68 m (from 1.28) with no reward for it, clubhead 84 mph from 17 mph hands (4.9×, a real golfer's 4–5×), and the release is passive (the wrist muscle does ~1 J; the body drives it). Balance does not come back yet (2026-11-05): after 1,500 balance-stage iterations it still falls 98% (90 mph, ball 114 mph), or 83% with braced legs (82 mph), both finishing at 0.42 m. The finish credit is zero everywhere below `finish_low` (0.8), so from a 0.42 m finish standing taller earns nothing; only the stand penalty pulls up. Adding `env.fall_penalty=50` to the braced run brings falls to 48%, and more training with finish credit from 0.6 m to ~16–20% (see the balance notes above). The loose-legs body stays faster (88–89 mph, ball 104–113 mph) but finds the depenetration launch (Known traps).
**Full arms (`env.arms=full`, 2026-11-21):** the stock arms couldn't hold a two-handed swing (Stage 4.5), so this body rebuilds them. Each shoulder gets its third axis: the locked rotY of the stock 3-axis shoulder joint, which is the upper arm's long axis at the rest pose. PhysX measures it about that rest axis, so in a raised arm it twists the forearm and swings the arm a little. It's ±75° at 45 N·m (about 2/3 of the other shoulder axes, as in people). The elbows straighten (lower limit −109°; the stock −90° left 19.5° of bend). Each hand gets a 2-axis wrist pivoting 7.2 cm before the hand's centre, where a person's wrist sits, so a wrist moves its hand ~5 cm at 40°. The lead wrist cocks about the club's toe-heel axis at address (−30..+80°, today's hinge) and bows/cups ±45°; the trail wrist moves ±60° on both axes; all four axes use `wrist_gear` (10 N·m). The club is welded to the lead hand, and the trail hand is held on the grip by `grip_spring`. That gives 27 actions and 18 + 3 × 27 = 99 observations, so no older checkpoint loads. Asset `golfer_<club>_full.usda` (`make_golfer_asset.py --arms full`); address `<club>_full.json` (`solve_address.py --arms full --lead_elbow_deg 5`): lead elbow 5°, trail hand on the grip 5.5 cm below the lead hand. It takes two passes, because the lead wrist's axes come from the solved club angle: solve, then rebuild the asset. Under random actions at 1000 N/m the trail hand stays 5.3 cm from its spot (stock arms 6–7 cm). A forearm roll joint was left out: a straight lead arm's twist rotates the forearm. **Results (2026-11-21, `docs/experiments.md`):** two-handed from scratch (`i7fa`: `grip_spring=1000` from iteration 0, `i7gr`'s recipe plus `no_cane`), model_2495 has 21% falls, 75% tall finishes, a 98 mph ball, 105 yd, smash 1.26, the trail hand on the grip through impact (~20 cm off it in the finish), 77% slices (face-to-path +7.6°). That's ~30 mph and 35 yd beyond any stock-arm golfer, with the lead foot planted, and it was still improving (86% → 21% falls in its last 500 iterations). Balance came later than on the stock arms: it fell on nearly every swing through both balance stages. One-handed on the same body (`i7fo`) it's far worse (91% falls, 70 mph, 100% slices): a relaxed straight arm and loose wrist hang the club onto the turf, so the cane rule ends most early episodes. Its strike stage also learns a 0.5 m jab, which collapses at the full stage's 1 m arming; a 400-iteration bridge at `impact_arm_dist=0.6` with the backswing paid to 1.8 m fixed that. 1,500 more iterations of `i7fa` (`i7fa4`–`i7fa6`): balance peaks at 2994 (6% falls, 93% tall, 96 mph, 102 yd, CoM off the feet 5% of the finish) and then drifts (11% falls by 3992), with the strike slipping toward thin (~45%) and the slice staying (+7–8°). In the finish the trail hand sits a steady ~22 cm off its spot, held off by the arm against ~220 N of spring (the 300 N cap binds on 1% of finish steps). **Fine-tunes from 2994 (2026-11-22):** `env.thin_sigma` (ball pay × exp(−(e/σ)²), e = how far the leading edge meets the ball above its tangent point, the thin line) at 5 mm fixes the strike in 500 iterations (`i7ft1`: 64% good, 27% fat, 9% thin, Corke's 63/32/5) with speed kept and the best two-handed balance (4% falls, 95% tall); by 3992 balance drifts back as in the control. `env.side_sigma` (ball pay × exp(−(landing side/σ)²) [m]) lands the shots on the line, but mostly by striking worse (heel, thin, topped, 12 mph slower), since a shorter shot has less distance to curve; `env.land_sigma` [deg] uses the landing point's angle off the line, atan(side/carry), so a shorter shot buys nothing: at 6° from `i7ft1` (thin kept, `i7la1`) the slice shrinks for real (face-to-path +6.6° → +4.4°, 21% slices, 63% fades, landing −0.6 yd) at ~3 mph and a fatter strike (45% fat); at 4° (`i7lb`) it swings the path further left into a pull-slice instead, so stricter is worse. A stiffer grip (2000 N/m, cap 600 N, `i7fg`) halves the finish gap (22 → 11.5 cm) at the same ~230 N, so the policy pushes the hand off with a fixed effort, and the stronger pull through the club costs ~14 mph of ball speed and the strike. Best two-handed iron: `i7la1` model_3992 (on line); `i7ft1` model_3493 is the fastest and best balanced.

**Ball + tee:** the ball is a real-spec sphere (r=21.34 mm, 45.9 g) with **gravity disabled**, and the tee is a **visual-only** prim with no collider. Phase 2 owns flight analytically, so PhysX only has to report the impact — a gravity-free ball needs no support, which also means the club can never strike the tee (a static tee would deflect the clubhead like a steel post). Off the tee (the 7-iron, Stage 4.5) the ball rests on the ground, still gravity-free.
**The ball has no collider.** PhysX never touches it: a strike is the clubhead's swept path passing it (after arming, inside the head's box) and the env writes the launch velocity from the smash-factor model. So nothing but the clubhead can move it — no kicks, no tunneling, no ghost hits. Address leaves 2 cm of air between clubface and ball.

**Target line:** the golfer faces **+X** with the ball in front of its chest, and a right-hander sends the ball to its **left, so the target direction is +Y** — not +X. The swing plane is perpendicular to the stance, which is easy to get backwards.
`solve_address.py` enforces this with a heading cost (shoulder and hip lines along +Y) and a no-overlap cost, plus an athletic stance: feet 0.40 m apart (shoulders 0.34), knees 25°, flat feet, weight over their middle. The first solve faced −Y: it built (w,x,y,z) quaternions for a sim that reads (x,y,z,w), which put upright-facing-+X outside its search space (see Known traps). After any address change, `scripts/check_address.py` must report **zero overlapping limbs**.

**Episode:** reset with agent addressing a teed ball; terminate on timeout (~5 s) or `post_impact_steps` after impact, **3 s by default: a held finish**. It was 1 s until 2026-11-16, and that window hid every fall (Known traps). **No fall termination in Stage 0** (see curriculum); add it only once standing is being rewarded.

**Parallelism:** start at 1,024 envs, scale toward 4,096 as VRAM allows. Headless, fixed small dt with substeps; increase solver iterations around contact. If impact is still noisy, detect clubhead-ball proximity and read velocities at that tick rather than trusting the collision impulse.

## Reward curriculum (the actual RL lesson — stage gates, not one reward)

1. **Stage 0 — swing at all:** reward **ball speed gained** (the real objective; the ball is gravity-free and starts at rest, so any speed is a genuine strike) + a **capped backswing** term for winding up + clubhead speed near the ball. **No alive bonus, no fall penalty, no fall termination** — swinging is the first goal, and if the golfer flings itself over to do it, fine.
   **Do NOT use a clubhead-to-ball distance ("approach") reward here.** The club starts at address ~6 cm from the ball, so distance reduction is optimised by standing perfectly still, and it penalises the backswing — the one thing a swing must do first. Measured: reward fell −1.2 → −7.0 and clubhead speed fell 13.2 → 7.2 mph as the policy learned to freeze. A distance term only makes sense if the club starts away from the ball.
   Contact detection needs **arming**: the clubhead must travel >0.25 m from the ball before a hit counts, or `contact_rate` reads 1.0 from iteration 0 and measures nothing.
   **Backwards hits are never paid:** back of the head, or the ball heading >90° off the target line, earns no ball speed and costs a one-off penalty (20). No golf shot goes backwards.
   **Current recipe (2026-10-30):** strike stage from scratch on defaults, then resume with `env.finish_frac=0.5 env.impact_arm_dist=1.0 env.backswing_cap=1.2` (strike counts only after a 1.0 m backswing; half the ball pay waits for a posture-scaled finish). Fresh per club: a driver policy does not transfer to an iron address.
   **Finish and aim knobs (2026-10-31, off by default):** `env.finish_low`/`env.finish_high` set the finish torso heights worth zero/full credit (0.6/1.0 m by default, which pays 22% for a kneel; 0.8 zeroes it). `env.aim_sigma` [deg] multiplies the ball pay by exp(−(offline/σ)²) on the horizontal launch angle, an early start on Stage 3. Measured from run 18, against a control run: σ = 15° halves the offline angle (+7° → +4°, still a push) at no cost in speed; the stricter finish added at most a few cm over what more training alone gave.
   **Balance, added once contact was met (2026-10-30):** penalties for the torso dropping below 0.95 m and for feet sliding >3 cm, with a 1 s post-impact finish. Off by default: from scratch it drowns the strike and the policy learns to stand still (measured, run 5). Turn it on only when resuming from a striking checkpoint: `--checkpoint <run>/model_N.pt env.stand_weight=10 env.feet_slip_weight=10`. Still no fall termination.
   **That stand penalty barely separates a fall from a stand** (measured 2026-11-05): the fall comes late in the finish window, so a faller pays ~2 points more against a ~140-point ball pay. `env.fall_penalty` (off by default) is a one-off cost when the torso first drops below `env.fall_height` (0.6 m, `record_swings.py`'s "fell" line); `Metrics/fell_rate` logs falls in training. At 50 (wrist, braced legs, against a control): falls 83% → 48% with no clubhead speed lost, while the control drifted to 95%. The swings that stay up are mostly crouched, 0.6–0.8 m.
   **Crouch, errant shots, control (2026-11-11, runs 34–54):** with finish credit from 0.6 m (`env.finish_low=0.6`) plus the fall penalty, more training alone takes the braced wrist golfer to ~16–20% falls with a 0.62 m finish, never tall. The crouch is in the downswing: it drops onto the trail knee (~110° at impact, head 1.30 → 1.00 m) in ~0.1 s, too short for per-second penalties to touch. Against a control, more posture weight, planted feet (`feet_slip_weight=50`), 50× energy cost and γ 0.995 were all within noise; `stand_weight=200` lifted the finish to 0.69 m. Errant shots: `env.aim_sigma=7` tightens direction (90% within 2.6° vs 4.0°); `env.strike_pow` (off by default) scales the ball pay by the sweet-spot factor^p, and at 1 lifts smash 1.30 → 1.35. Two rules learned the hard way:
   - Multiplicative quality terms (aim, strike, finish) and fixed penalties only go on once the shots are decent: on a fresh policy they shrank the ball pay below the fall penalty and it learned to stand still and not swing (run 51).
   - A threshold teaches only where a good share of attempts land on each side: a fall line at 0.75 m, which no swing cleared, made the penalty constant and falls went 22% → 58%.
   The wrist golfer trained with braced legs from iteration 0 (`leg_damping_scale=1.0` from scratch) learns a taller downswing (head 1.20 m, trail knee 86° at impact, offline 1.1°, 75 mph); the crouch came from learning the first swing on loose legs. Its balance stage (run 29's recipe with finish credit from 0.6 m, then `fall_penalty=30`, kept below the immediate half of the ball pay) finishes standing tall, knees straight, on 48% of swings after 500 iterations and 66% after 1,000 (falls 25%): the most promising lineage, at a cost of ~15 mph against the loose-legs body, and its strike quality slips as balance improves (smash 1.20 → 1.12). The misses sit toward the heel (~1.8 cm) and scatter ~1 cm up and down the face (`Metrics/strike_heel_cm`, `strike_high_cm`, also in `record_swings.py`). `env.strike_bonus` (off by default) pays ball × (1 + bonus × sweet-spot factor): it only adds, so it can't make standing still pay. At 1 (run 57, against a control): smash 1.11 → 1.16, heel miss 1.8 → 1.1 cm, ball +4 mph, but falls 21% → 32%, probably because the bigger ball pay shrinks the fixed fall penalty's relative weight.
   **Fall penalty saturates; exploration noise was the ceiling (2026-11-16, runs 59–66).** From run 58 (21% falls), a sweep of `fall_penalty` 30/40/60/100 (500 iters each): 40 holds falls at 25% where 30 drifts to 32%, but 60 and 100 fall just as often and cost strike quality; more training at 40 didn't help. The falls are late, slow sit-backs (torso crosses 0.6 m 0.7–1.0 s after impact, mostly backward). What worked was the action noise: rsl_rl's learned std (`distribution.std_param`) barely moves with `entropy_coef=0` (0.49 → 0.47 over 1,500 iters, on actions clipped to ±1). Resuming run 59 with it scaled to 0.25 (`scripts/set_action_std.py`) gave 18% falls, 69% tall finishes, finish torso 0.93 m, smash 1.24 and ball 80 mph, against 29% / 52% / 0.78 m / 1.15 / 74 mph at 0.47 (run 65 vs run 64); 0.12 cleaned strikes as well but balance stalled (25%). Those balance numbers used the 1 s finish window; with 3 s, run 65's lineage falls on every swing (next paragraph).
   **Feet, tempo, and the window (2026-11-16, runs 67–74, from run 65):** `env.lead_foot_weight` (lead foot slide beyond 3 cm or lift beyond `lead_foot_lift_free`; the trail foot may lift and pivot) did nothing at 50 or 150: about 0.2 s after impact the lead leg kicks up (0.1–0.55 m) and out (0.3–1.0 m), a counterbalance, not a slip (friction is 1.0). `env.motor_noise` is signal-dependent motor noise (each joint's torque error SD = that fraction of the torque, correlated over `motor_noise_tau` = 50 ms; Harris & Wolpert), meant to make rushed, forceful swings inaccurate. At 0.1 and 0.2 it only cost speed and balance, and the timing didn't move (top 0.22 s, downswing 0.11 s). Neither did a discount of `agent.algorithm.gamma=0.999`, which removes the pressure of γ 0.99 (a strike 0.87 s later is worth 0.59 at 0.99, 0.95 at 0.999), with or without noise. Timing and the finish strategy are not something a 500-iteration fine-tune reshapes. Tempo is logged as `Metrics/top_time` / `downswing_time`.
   **Holding the finish (2026-11-16, runs 75–78, from run 67 at the 3 s window):** `env.hold_weight` pays per second after impact × the upright share (so a later fall earns more even when every swing falls), and `env.fall_terminate` ends the episode at the fall (the spec's "once standing is rewarded"). At hold 20, and at 20 or 50 with termination, nothing moved: 100% fall, median 1.25–1.33 s after impact, episode length flat for 500 iterations, no speed traded for balance. This lineage's fall is built into its swing; next is learning the swing and finish together from scratch.
   **Swing and finish learned together (2026-11-16):** from scratch, the balance stages run with the 3 s window, hold 20, `fall_terminate` and std 0.25 from their first iteration, then `fall_penalty=40`. `env.takeaway_weight` (off by default) charges clubhead speed above `takeaway_speed` (4.5 m/s, ~10 mph) while the club moves away from the ball before impact. With it at 10 from iteration 0 (chain `takeaway`, 1,700 iterations): **75% of swings hold a 3 s finish, 74% standing tall**, lead foot nearly planted (0.29 m), 53.5 mph clubhead, ball 63 mph, smash 1.23, tempo 2.2:1 with a shorter 1.35 m backswing. Without it (chain `bal_ref`): 69 mph, ball 90 mph, smash 1.37, 1.1° offline, but 87% fall within 3 s (median 2.2 s after impact). ## Repo conventions

- Layout: `envs/` (env + reward code), `assets/` (USD/URDF — small ones committable), `scripts/` (train/eval/replay entrypoints), `flight/` (Phase 2 analytic model + unit tests — pure Python, testable without Isaac), `docs/` (versions, experiments log), `runs/` (gitignored).
- `.gitignore`: `runs/`, checkpoints (`*.pt`, `*.onnx`), TensorBoard events, `__pycache__/`, large USD/binary assets, Omniverse cache dirs.
- The flight model is ordinary physics code: write it test-first (known inputs → known carry distances from published golf data) before wiring it to Isaac.
- Commit small, push each session. Experiments without a logged config/result didn't happen.
- **Archive every stage's swings:** `uv run python scripts/archive_run.py <run dir> --stage stageN --note "..."` films each checkpoint in Kit (down the line, replaying the run's body settings (`club`, `wrist`, `muscle`, `damping_scale`, `leg_damping_scale`, `impact_arm_dist`, `max_depenetration_velocity`) from its `env.yaml`), stacks them into `progression.mp4`, saves the swing viewer, and adds a row to `media/index.md`. Only `progression.mp4` and `swings.html` go into `media/` (tracked; logs/ isn't): ~0.7 MB and 2–4 MB. The single clips stay in the run's `videos/play/`. Use `--existing` for runs whose env has since changed. Several run dirs (a chain in training order, each resumed from the last) film as one progression across the whole chain, each run replayed with its own body settings; the viewer is the last run's. Past 4 checkpoints the clips wrap onto two rows (`--max_ckpts 6` for a chain). `--iters 2994 3493 3992` films exactly those checkpoints.
  **Shot tracer:** any rendered run (GUI or `--video`) draws env 0's computed flight (`flight/`, Phase 2 above) as a red line, played back over `env.tracer_flight_s` (2 s) from the strike, and keeps it until the next strike (`env.tracer`, on by default; headless training never creates it; needs `env.flight`). The sim ball doesn't follow it: moving the ball would change what the policy observes.
- **Distances in yards** (carry, side, curve, apex), like speeds in mph: `YD` from `golf_env_cfg.py`, display only; internally metres.
- **Speeds are reported in mph** (clubhead and ball): TensorBoard `Metrics/*_speed_mph`, `record_swings.py`, the swing viewer, `media/index.md`, `docs/experiments.md`. Everything internal (env, cfg, rewards, recorded swing data) stays m/s; convert with `MPH` from `golf_env_cfg.py` only where it is displayed. Direction is the **offline angle** in degrees, + = right of target (a push or slice for this right-hander). Backswing is the farthest the clubhead gets from the ball before impact, in metres. Spread (errant shots): `record_swings.py` also reports the 90th-percentile offline angle, the 10th-percentile smash, and the share of finishes with the torso at 0.9 m or higher.
- **Watch the policy before trusting a reward curve.** `scripts/record_swings.py <run dir> physics=isaacsim_physx` rolls out every checkpoint headless and writes one self-contained HTML viewer to `<run>/swings/` (scrub, slow-mo, camera presets, which body part struck the ball). For a Kit render: `isaaclab play --rl_library rsl_rl --task Isaac-Golf-Swing-Direct --num_envs 1 --checkpoint <path/to/model_N.pt> --video --video_length 300 physics=isaacsim_physx` — needs the `video` extra (in `pyproject.toml`); 3.0's `play` has no `--load_run`.

## Known traps (pre-answered — no session relitigates them)

- **Don't simulate ball aerodynamics in PhysX** — no Magnus lift; the flight model owns everything after impact.
- **Don't learn grasping** — weld the club.
- **Don't train with the GUI open** — headless, or it will crawl and eat RAM.
- **Don't end-to-end the whole golf round** — hierarchy per Phase 3.
- **Don't trust raw contact impulses at impact** — small dt/substeps, or sample clubhead velocity at proximity. Measured (`scripts/check_impact.py`): at 120 Hz PhysX misses most strikes above ~11 mph and all at 56–89 mph; sweep CCD is CPU-only, speculative CCD does nothing on GPU articulation links, and a big ball contact offset invents strikes on near misses. So the ball doesn't collide at all: contact is the clubhead's swept path (reliable at any speed) and the launch is computed (smash factor, Phase 2 above).
- **Don't start with a sparse ball-in-hole reward** — curriculum above.
- **Club welds to `left_hand` only** (lead hand for a right-handed swing). The humanoid *does* have `left_hand`/`right_hand` bodies — 4 cm spheres on fixed joints, zero DoF, ideal anchors. Welding **both** hands closes a kinematic loop (torso → left arm → club → right arm → torso) which PhysX must solve as an extra constraint at only 4 position iterations: sag, jitter, blowup. The trail (right) hand is kept on the grip by a **reward penalty**, not a constraint — tree stays intact and the policy learns to hold on. The soft version is `env.grip_spring` (Stage 4.5): a force, not a joint, off by default.
The **grip cluster never self-collides** (club, both hands, both forearms: `FilteredPairsAPI` in `make_golfer_asset.py`). Fat capsules converging on one grip always overlap, and with no wrists the shaft crosses the lead forearm permanently. PhysX only skips jointed pairs, so before the filter it flung those bodies apart at every reset.
- **Don't fork blind:** env logic lives in `locomotion_direct_env.py`, not the humanoid files.
- **Don't copy Isaac Lab 2.x tutorials verbatim** — 3.0 moved asset data behind `.torch` accessors, dropped `--headless`, and dropped the `-v0` task suffix.
- **Isaac Lab 3.0 quaternions are (x, y, z, w)** — root poses, `InitialStateCfg.rot`, body quats, `isaaclab.utils.math`. USD (`Gf.Quatf`, joint `localRot`) is still (w, x, y, z). Mixing them up doesn't crash; it silently rotates things. That is how the first address solve came out facing the wrong way.
- **Version drift:** Isaac Lab ↔ Isaac Sim ↔ driver versions get pinned once working; upgrades are deliberate, between phases, never mid-training.
- **Depenetration launch (measured 2026-11-11):** the loose-legs wrist golfer learned to drive a body into an overlap so PhysX pushes it out at the robot's `max_depenetration_velocity` (then 10 m/s): it flies 2–4 m up after impact, which dodges the fall and stand penalties and maxes the finish credit. Replaying at 1 m/s removes most of it, so **`env.max_depenetration_velocity` now defaults to 1.0** (runs 57+; earlier runs trained at 10, and `archive_run.py` replays each run at its own value). Resets still settle: no limb overlaps at reset in 512 jittered envs; the reset jitter can push a foot or the club's toe up to ~3 cm into the ground, which now takes ~3 steps to push out instead of 1. Run 55's braced swing scored the same at 1 and 10 m/s across two seeds. Still check torso peak height (address is 1.14 m) before trusting a finish or fall number.
- **Measure balance over a 3 s finish (2026-11-16).** With the old 1 s post-impact window, run 67 scored 17% falls and 74% tall finishes; rolled out for 3 s, every swing falls (18% by 1.0 s, 67% by 1.5 s, 96% by 2.0 s, median 1.3 s after impact). The window and the finish credit ended before the golfer hit the ground. `post_impact_steps` defaults to 180 now. Never judge balance on a window shorter than the slowest fall.
- **Read the club at impact, not at the step's end (fixed 2026-11-18).** Near the ball the club turns ~20° per 1/60 s step, so the end-of-step velocity reads the path ~half a step late. Until 2026-11-18 `path_deg` used it: `balfix3` read path −8.0°, attack −0.3°, face-to-path +10° (95% slices); interpolated to the moment the head passes the ball (as the head's orientation already was) it is −4.8°, −9.4° and +6.7° (70% slices). Face-to-path and slice numbers logged before then overstate the slice by ~3°. The fix also reads the speed into the face at impact: ball pay +1–2%.
- **RAM: at most two Isaac processes at once.** Two trainings plus a Kit video render (`archive_run.py`) ran the 32 GB machine out of memory and Claude Code reaped the background jobs (2026-11-11); one training plus a render still dipped below 4 GB free (a training holds ~4 GB, a render peaks near 9 GB). Evaluate with `record_swings.py` (headless) while training; film when nothing else is running.
